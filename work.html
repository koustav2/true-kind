Our Work

Five programs, chosen for how they compound.

A trained young person earns. An earning mother keeps her daughter in school. A school with a working computer lab produces the next trainee. We picked these five because each one makes the other four go further.

Skilling

Skill Development & Employment

Trade and digital-service training designed around what employers within travelling distance are actually hiring for. Batches run year-round; placement support continues for six months after the certificate, because that's when most people quietly fall out of the system.

Help run a batch →
Livelihoods

Women's Empowerment

Savings collectives, micro-enterprise training and the unglamorous part nobody funds — market linkages. Products only pay if there's a buyer, so we work on pricing, bulk supply and retailer introductions alongside the skills themselves.

Support a collective →
Education

Digital Education Access

Device-light computer and internet literacy labs run alongside government schools. Low hardware requirements on purpose — a lab that needs constant power and new machines is a lab that stops running by year two.

Teach a session →
Health

Health & Safety Camps

Preventive-care camps, workplace safety training and first-aid literacy for communities a long way from the nearest clinic. Screening catches the conditions that are cheap to manage early and ruinous to treat late.

Join a camp →
Environment

Environmental Sustainability

Neighbourhood tree-planting, plastic-reduction drives and environment education, co-run with residents. Saplings are counted at planting and again a year later — survival rate is the number that matters, not the number in the ground.

Plant with us →

Every program, same rules

What a True Kind program has to have

If a proposal can't meet these four, we don't run it — however good the idea sounds.

A named local partner

Someone who lives there, is accountable for delivery, and can tell us when we're wrong.

A follow-up window

A defined point months later when we go back and check what actually held.

A number we can be wrong about

One measure, agreed at the start, that can come back worse than we hoped.

A public budget line

What it costs, and what share of that reaches the community rather than the office.
